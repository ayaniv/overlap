Time zone meeting planner: find the hours your team overlaps

Overlap Clock is a free world clock built for one question: when is everyone on my team actually working at the same time? It draws your home time zone in the center and every other city as a ring around it, so overlapping working hours across time zones are something you see, not something you calculate.

Open Overlap Clock

What Overlap Clock is

Most time zone converters give you a table of numbers to scan. Overlap Clock gives you a single radial clock instead. Each city you add becomes a ring, each ring carries a colored arc for that city's working hours, and a dot on every ring marks its current time. Where the arcs line up, everyone is available; where they don't, someone is asleep. It runs in any modern browser on desktop or mobile.

How to find overlapping working hours across time zones

  1. Add your cities. Add the city of every teammate, client or friend you need to meet with. Each one gets its own ring and color, and you can set its working hours to match how that person really works.
  2. Read the arcs. The colored arcs show each city's working hours against your own day. The stretch where all of them overlap is your shared meeting window.
  3. Pick a time. Drag the clock face, or use the arrow keys, to preview any time across every ring at once. Or press Find overlap to jump to the next time when the most cities are inside their working hours at once.
  4. Schedule it. Once a time works, schedule the meeting straight to your own Google Calendar after signing in with Google. Overlap Clock only creates that event, and never reads your other calendar events.

Share the exact view with a link

Your cities, colors and working hours are encoded into the page's link. Press Share and anyone who opens that link sees the same clock you do, with no account and nothing to set up. Your setup also stays in your browser's local storage, so it's there the next time you come back.

Who it's for

  • Distributed and remote teams spread across several time zones.
  • Freelancers and agencies working with overseas clients.
  • Anyone planning a call with friends or family in another country.

Free, open source, no account

Overlap Clock is free and open source under the MIT license, with its code on GitHub. There's no signup and no account, and the developer runs no server that stores your clock setup. See the privacy policy for exactly what the app touches.

Also a Chrome extension

The same world clock is available as a Chrome extension, in a toolbar popup with a Share action and a shortcut to open the full Overlap Clock web app, which is where Google Calendar scheduling lives.
