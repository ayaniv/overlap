Overlap Clock

Plan meetings across time zones by looking at one clock

Overlap Clock puts your local time in the middle and every city you work with on a ring around it. Each ring is colored during that city's working hours. Where the colors line up, everyone is at work, and that's when to meet.

Open Overlap Clock Free. No account.

How to find a meeting time

  1. Add the cities

    Add a city for each person you meet with. Every city gets its own ring and color, and you can change its working hours for anyone who keeps a different day.

  2. Look for where the colors line up

    The colored part of each ring is that city's working day. The stretch where every ring is colored is the time your whole group is at work.

  3. Pick a time

    Drag around the clock, or use the arrow keys, to move every city's time at once. Or press Find overlap to jump to the next time when the most cities are at work together.

  4. Put it on the calendar

    Schedule the meeting and it's added to your Google Calendar after you sign in with Google. Overlap Clock creates that one event and never reads the rest of your calendar.

Send someone your exact view

The cities, colors and hours you set are saved in the page's link. Press Share, send the link, and the other person opens the same clock without signing up. Your setup is also kept in your browser, so it's still there the next time you open Overlap Clock.

Who uses it

Remote teams spread across continents, freelancers with clients abroad, and anyone trying to find a reasonable hour to call family in another country.

Free and open source

Overlap Clock is free, needs no account, and its code is on GitHub under the MIT license. The developer doesn't run a server that stores your cities or hours. The privacy policy lists exactly what the app touches.

In your browser toolbar

The Chrome extension opens the same clock from your toolbar, with Share and a button to open the full web app. Scheduling to Google Calendar lives in the web app.
